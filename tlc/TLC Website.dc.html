Mindfulness

Erik Delgado, School PsychologistMay 10, 20156 min read

Mindfulness has its roots in the teaching of the Buddha and other Eastern religious traditions. However, in the 1970s it began to be thought of as a cognitive ability, a personality trait, a mindset, a quality of consciousness, and life orientation or a set of psychological skills.

Mindfulness is composed of three main subskills: mindful awareness, mindful responsivity, and mindful effort. These skills can be used and developed separately; however, it is only when they are used in combination that mindfulness is fully practiced, especially in stressful moments.

Mindful awareness is the ability to actively observe what is going on here and now within yourself and your environment. Mindful responsivity refers to how we respond to the present moment: being friendly to ourselves and others as we react. Mindful effort is the ability to engage in intentional action, especially when faced with a difficult situation.

Research has demonstrated positive effects on social skills and social emotional learning in students and parents. When practiced by students, studies suggest improvements ranging from happiness to academic success to physical health.

“

In summary mindfulness is being attentive to what is happening right now, while being kind to yourself and those around you, even when it is very difficult.

— Erik Delgado

In order for you to learn these skills, here are two exercises: mindful breathing and mindful STOP.

Mindful breathing

You can practice mindful breathing every day, anytime, anywhere. It means paying attention to your breath in the here and now and being kind toward whatever you notice along the way.

Sit comfortably in your chair with both feet on the floor, place your hands on your lap, and close your eyes. Take 3 deep breaths. Feel the air entering your body from your nose or mouth, and your stomach going in and out as it comes and goes. Practice it for 30 seconds and repeat one more time.

Mindful STOP

Mindful breathing is a good tool to start building mindfulness; however, you will need a different skill when you encounter difficult challenges. STOP is an acronym to help you remember the steps.
