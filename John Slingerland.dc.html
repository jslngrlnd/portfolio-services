Featured works

Clinical AI · platform architecture · shipped features

01 — Flagship / platform

From admin checklist to point of care assistant

A clinical assistant used by primary care physicians had grown into four disconnected experiences and feature bloat. I led the visioning for its redesign — workshops, an end-to-end vision prototype, then the roadmap, team structure and architecture that carried it to a shipped MVP.

02 — Feature

Ninety seconds before the exam room

Helping Physicians with back to back patient encounters gain context when time is precious. I designed a briefing they could scan, what happened, what needs attention today, and what the model wasn’t sure about.

See the case study

02 — Feature

Helping physicians gain context when time is precious

The pre-visit brief was working as a concept and failing as a page — clinicians were being asked to read where they needed to scan. I ran design experiments, studied how mature products in adjacent industries solve the same problem, and shipped three information-architecture improvements into the alpha build.

See the case study

03 — Care coordination

Improving the care for hospitalized patients

Health Navigators were tracking hospital admissions for One Medical's highest-risk patients in a copy-paste spreadsheet. I led the design of an Admissions Board built into the EHR, aligning multiple teams on where it should live and what care teams needed at a glance: clear ownership, service standards and next steps.

See the case study

03 — Enterprise UX

We lost real money because our software was too hard to turn on

Installing AppDynamics agents took hundreds of steps and a company-wide naming scheme invented up front. An engineer reframed it for me as a grouping problem the software could solve itself. We shipped it as two commands and three steps.

See the case study

04 — Enterprise UX

Fifteen screens and a box of ticks to grant one person access

Rigid role-based permissions were costing AppDynamics admins time and creating security risk — and nobody could see what they’d actually granted. We replaced ticking boxes with writing rules: an entity-based access model with attributes, tagging and inheritance, validated through research, prototyping and iterative testing.

See the case study
